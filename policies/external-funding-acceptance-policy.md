# EXTERNAL FUNDING ACCEPTANCE POLICY

*restore280 Institute — Adopted by the Board of Directors by Written Consent, effective October 1, 2026*

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs the acceptance and rejection of all external funding by restore280 Institute (the "Corporation"), including grants, donations, sponsorships, and in-kind contributions. Its purpose is to ensure that external funding furthers the Corporation's exempt purposes without compromising its editorial independence, research integrity, or tax-exempt status. This policy applies to all funding received from sources other than the Corporation's own capital generation activities.

---

## 2. General Acceptance Standards

### 2.1 Permitted Funding

The Corporation may accept external funding that:

(a) furthers one or more of the Corporation's exempt purposes;

(b) does not impose restrictions that conflict with the Corporation's editorial or research independence;

(c) does not create impermissible private benefit or private inurement under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code;

(d) does not constitute an excess benefit transaction under Section 4958; and

(e) does not create a material reputational, legal, or compliance risk for the Corporation.

### 2.2 Prohibited Funding

The Corporation shall not accept funding:

(a) conditioned on the Corporation reaching a specific editorial conclusion, omitting specific information, or modifying published research;

(b) from any entity or individual for whom fossil fuel extraction, processing, or distribution represents a material part of their commercial activity, where acceptance would create a material conflict with the Corporation's mission;

(c) from any entity or individual currently subject to unresolved legal proceedings involving fraud, corruption, or willful environmental violations, where acceptance would create material reputational risk to the Corporation;

(d) that constitutes a quid pro quo arrangement in violation of applicable law; or

(e) that would require the Corporation to engage in activity inconsistent with its exempt purposes or Bylaws.

### 2.3 Borderline Decisions

Funding acceptance decisions that are not clearly permitted or prohibited under Sections 2.1 and 2.2 shall be escalated to the Board for determination. The Executive Director shall document the basis for any borderline acceptance or rejection decision.

---

## 3. Editorial and Research Independence

### 3.1 Independence Standard

The Corporation's editorial and research positions are determined solely by evidence and the Corporation's epistemological standards. No funder shall have the right to direct, approve, suppress, or modify the Corporation's editorial or research output as a condition of funding.

### 3.2 Permitted Donor Restrictions

Donors may restrict the use of their contribution to a specific program area or exempt purpose of the Corporation, provided the restriction:

(a) does not require a specific editorial or research conclusion;

(b) does not require the Corporation to omit material information; and

(c) is consistent with the Corporation's stated exempt purposes.

### 3.3 Prohibited Donor Restrictions

The Corporation shall not accept funding subject to restrictions that:

(a) require the Corporation to reach or avoid a specific editorial conclusion;

(b) require the Corporation to name or avoid naming specific legislation, policies, or public figures;

(c) require advance funder approval of content before publication; or

(d) give the funder any editorial role or veto right over the Corporation's research or content.

---

## 4. Due Diligence

### 4.1 Threshold

Before accepting any single contribution of $10,000 or more, the Executive Director shall conduct reasonable due diligence on the funding source sufficient to confirm that acceptance does not violate Section 2.2 and does not present material reputational risk to the Corporation.

### 4.2 Board Member Contributions

The due diligence requirement of Section 4.1 is satisfied for any contribution from a then-serving director by that director's existing annual conflict of interest disclosure and independence vetting under the Corporation's Conflict of Interest Policy. No additional source due diligence is required for contributions from directors. This carveout applies only to the due diligence requirement; the acknowledgment, disclosure, donor-restriction, and conflict of interest provisions of this policy apply in full to contributions from directors.

### 4.3 Documentation

Due diligence findings shall be documented and retained as part of the Corporation's funding records for at least seven years.

### 4.4 Aggregation

Contributions from the same source that individually fall below $10,000 but cumulatively reach $10,000 within a single fiscal year shall trigger the due diligence requirement upon reaching the threshold. This aggregation rule does not apply to director contributions, which are exempt from due diligence under Section 4.2 regardless of cumulative amount.

---

## 5. Conflicts of Interest

Any funding acceptance decision in which a director, officer, or key employee has a material Financial Interest shall be subject to the conflict of interest procedures in the Corporation's Conflict of Interest Policy. The Interested Person shall recuse from the acceptance decision.

---

## 6. Gift Processing and Acknowledgment

### 6.1 Written Acknowledgment

The Corporation shall provide written acknowledgment of all charitable contributions as required by Section 170(f) of the Internal Revenue Code. For contributions of $250 or more, the acknowledgment shall state whether any goods or services were provided in exchange and, if so, a good-faith estimate of their value.

### 6.2 Quid Pro Quo Contributions

For any contribution where the donor receives goods or services in exchange with a fair market value exceeding $75, the Corporation shall provide the written disclosure required by Section 6115 of the Internal Revenue Code, stating the fair market value of the goods or services provided and the amount of the contribution that is deductible.

### 6.3 In-Kind Contributions

In-kind contributions of property shall be accepted only if the property furthers the Corporation's exempt purposes or can be converted to cash without material cost. The Corporation shall provide written acknowledgment of in-kind contributions but shall not assign a dollar value to them in the acknowledgment; valuation is the donor's responsibility. In-kind contributions with an asserted value of $500 or more shall require the donor to complete IRS Form 8283 as applicable.

### 6.4 Anonymous Contributions

The Corporation may accept anonymous contributions consistent with applicable law. Anonymous contributions shall be subject to the same acceptance standards as identified contributions. For anonymous contributions of $10,000 or more, the Executive Director shall document the basis for accepting the contribution without identifying the source.

---

## 7. Disclosure

The Corporation shall disclose all funding sources as required by the Internal Revenue Code and applicable state law, including on Form 990. The Corporation shall maintain records of all contributions sufficient to support required disclosures for at least seven years.

---

## 8. Amendment

This policy may be amended by majority Board action pursuant to Article VII of the Bylaws.
