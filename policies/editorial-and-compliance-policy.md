# EDITORIAL AND COMPLIANCE POLICY

*restore280 Institute — Adopted by the Board of Directors by Written Consent, effective October 1, 2026*

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs the editorial standards, lobbying expenditure classification and tracking procedures, and content compliance requirements for all content produced and published by restore280 Institute (the "Corporation"). It applies to all video content, written research, interactive tools, social media, and any other public-facing communication produced by or on behalf of the Corporation.

Whether a given content topic is selected for production at all, and how much of the Corporation's production capacity it receives, is governed by the Strategic Prioritization and Resource Allocation Policy. This policy has no role in that selection decision. It governs only how content already selected under that policy is produced, reviewed, and published.

---

## 2. Nonpartisan Editorial Standard

### 2.1 Political, Not Partisan

The Corporation's editorial position is political, not partisan. The Corporation analyzes and reports on structural and institutional failures, policy mechanisms, and systemic causes of the conditions it works to address. Accuracy is the editorial standard, not symmetry between competing political positions. The Corporation does not balance accurate analysis against inaccurate claims in the name of fairness.

### 2.2 Structural Analysis Framework

Content produced by the Corporation shall:

(a) locate failure at the level of institutional design rather than individual choice or moral failing;

(b) refrain from attributing motives to named individuals or organizations; motives are not subject to verification and are outside the Corporation's editorial scope;

(c) document the actions and public positions of public figures as matters of public record; and

(d) analyze structures and systems using the best available evidence, including peer-reviewed research, government data, and verified primary sources.

### 2.3 Prohibited Editorial Positions

Content produced by the Corporation shall not:

(a) constitute political campaign intervention, as defined and evaluated under the standard set forth in Section 4 of this policy, including the absolute prohibition in Section 4.1 and the seven-factor analysis in Section 4.2;

(b) attribute conclusions to motives where evidence supports only documentation of actions; or

(c) present consumer-level individual action as a primary or sufficient response to systemic problems.

## 3. Lobbying Expenditure Classification and Tracking

### 3.1 501(h) Election

The Corporation has filed IRS Form 5768 to make the expenditure-based lobbying election under Sections 501(h) and 4911 of the Internal Revenue Code. Under this election, the Corporation's lobbying activity is measured by expenditure rather than by a substantiality standard, subject to the limits set forth in Section 3.4.

### 3.2 Definitions

For purposes of this policy:

**(a) Direct Lobbying** means any communication with a member or employee of a legislative body, or with any government official who may participate in the formulation of legislation, that refers to specific legislation and reflects a view on that legislation.

**(b) Grassroots Lobbying** means any communication with the general public that refers to specific legislation, reflects a view on that legislation, and includes a call to action. A call to action means any statement that directly encourages the recipient to contact a legislator or government official, provides contact information for a legislator or government official, or provides a mechanism for contacting a legislator or government official.

**(c) Issue Advocacy** means communication on a legislative or policy issue that does not include a call to action, or that does not refer to specific legislation.

**(d) Lobbying Expenditures** means all expenditures for direct lobbying and grassroots lobbying as defined above.

### 3.3 Classification Methodology

The Corporation classifies lobbying expenditures consistent with the cost allocation rules of Treasury Regulation § 56.4911-3.

**(a) Apportionment for separable segments.** Where a piece of content contains a call to action as an identifiable, separable segment distinct from non-lobbying content (for example, a CTA segment within the Corporation's standard video structure of Hook, Hidden System, Stakes, CTAs, and Return), only the production and distribution cost reasonably allocable to that segment shall be classified as a lobbying expenditure. Allocation shall be made on a reasonable basis, such as the proportion of total runtime occupied by the CTA segment, applied to total production and distribution cost for the piece.

**(b) Non-severability override.** If the lobbying message is not contained in a readily identifiable, separable segment, including where the narrative structure of the content is built to advance the legislative ask such that earlier segments cannot reasonably be characterized as independent of the call to action, the entire cost of producing and distributing that content shall be classified as a lobbying expenditure. The Executive Director shall document, for each piece of content containing a call to action, whether subsection (a) or subsection (b) applies and the basis for that determination.

**(c) Direct versus grassroots.** Where content could be classified as either direct or grassroots lobbying, it shall be classified as grassroots lobbying for expenditure tracking purposes, as the grassroots limit is more restrictive.

**(d) Conservative default.** Where the Executive Director cannot make a reasonably confident separability determination under subsections (a) and (b), the non-severability override in subsection (b) shall apply and the full cost shall be classified as a lobbying expenditure.

### 3.4 Expenditure Limits

Under the 501(h) election, the Corporation's annual lobbying expenditure limits are as follows:

**(a) Total Lobbying Limit:** 20% of the first $500,000 of exempt purpose expenditures, plus 15% of the next $500,000, plus 10% of the next $500,000, plus 5% of any remaining exempt purpose expenditures, up to a maximum of $1,000,000.

**(b) Grassroots Lobbying Limit:** 25% of the total lobbying limit calculated under subsection (a).

The Executive Director shall monitor cumulative lobbying expenditures against these limits quarterly and shall alert the Board if cumulative expenditures reach 75% of either limit in any fiscal year.

### 3.5 Required Call-to-Action Components

All policy intervention content published by the Corporation shall include a call to action satisfying at least one of the following components:

**(a) Specific legislation:** The call to action shall name the specific legislation or regulatory mechanism being referenced, including bill number and primary sponsors where applicable.

**(b) Representative engagement:** The call to action shall include an explicit prompt directing the audience to contact their elected representative regarding their position on the named legislation.

**(c) Existing organizations:** The call to action shall identify one to two organizations already engaged in advocacy or implementation related to the named legislation, with a one-line description of each.

Content that does not satisfy at least one of the above components shall not be published as policy intervention content. Consumer-level individual action items are not a permitted substitute for any of the three components.

### 3.6 Expenditure Tracking

The Executive Director shall maintain a lobbying expenditure log recording, for each piece of content classified as a lobbying expenditure:

(a) the title and publication date of the content;

(b) the specific legislation referenced;

(c) the classification (direct or grassroots);

(d) whether Section 3.3(a) (apportionment) or Section 3.3(b) (full cost) was applied, and the basis for that determination;

(e) the total production and distribution expenditure allocated to that content; and

(f) the cumulative lobbying expenditure total for the fiscal year to date.

The lobbying expenditure log shall be included in the quarterly financial summary provided to the Board under Section 8.4 of the Bylaws.

## 4. Political Campaign Intervention Prohibition

### 4.1 Absolute Prohibition

The Corporation shall not participate or intervene in any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public office, consistent with Section 9.3 of the Bylaws and Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. This prohibition is absolute.

### 4.2 Seven-Factor Screening

All content produced by the Corporation shall be reviewed against the seven-factor test established in Revenue Ruling 2007-41, 2007-1 C.B. 1421, before publication. The seven factors are:

(a) Whether the communication identifies one or more candidates for public office;

(b) Whether the communication expresses approval or disapproval for one or more candidates' positions or actions;

(c) Whether the communication is delivered close in time to an election;

(d) Whether the communication makes reference to voting or an election;

(e) Whether the issue addressed has been raised as an issue distinguishing candidates for a given office;

(f) Whether the communication is part of an ongoing series by the Corporation on the same issue, made independently of the timing of any election; and

(g) Whether the timing of the communication and the identification of any candidate are related to a non-electoral event such as a scheduled legislative vote.

The Executive Director shall document the seven-factor review for each piece of content, noting which factors are present or absent and the conclusion reached. Content that triggers factors (a) or (b) shall not be published without Board approval and written legal review. Content that triggers factors (c), (d), or (e) without triggering (a) or (b) shall be reviewed under Section 4.3.

### 4.3 Election-Proximity Content Restriction

The Corporation shall not publish new content addressing electoral processes, electoral reform, voting rights, democratic accountability mechanisms, or any topic that has been raised as an issue distinguishing candidates in a pending election during the sixty (60) day period immediately preceding any general election (the "Restricted Period"). For federal elections held on the first Tuesday following the first Monday in November, the Restricted Period covers September and October of each election year.

This restriction applies to new content only. Previously published content that remains available through the Corporation's platforms during a Restricted Period is not subject to this restriction.

The Executive Director shall maintain a content calendar noting the Restricted Period for each applicable election cycle and shall flag any content scheduled for publication during a Restricted Period for Board review before publication.

### 4.4 Documentation

For any content published during the sixty-day window preceding a primary or general election, the Executive Director shall document:

(a) the complete seven-factor analysis under Section 4.2;

(b) the conclusion that the content does not constitute political campaign intervention; and

(c) the basis for that conclusion.

This documentation shall be retained for at least seven years as part of the Corporation's compliance records.

## 5. Content Standards

### 5.1 Factual Verification and Sourcing Standard

Every specific factual claim, fact, or figure in content published by the Corporation shall be derived from a document resulting from a scientific, empirical, or analytical process that has been made publicly available for review. Qualifying sources include peer-reviewed academic publications, government data and reports, and publications from established research organizations that publish their methodology (for example, the Pew Research Center or the Economist Intelligence Unit). A source qualifies under this standard if its underlying methodology is publicly available for independent scrutiny, regardless of whether the Corporation itself publishes a companion research document for a given piece of content. For quantitative claims, the underlying data and methodology of the source shall be documented in the Corporation's internal source records. The Executive Director is responsible for maintaining a verification process adequate to this standard.

### 5.2 Optional Research Documentation

The Corporation may, at its discretion, publish a research document or whitepaper alongside any piece of content to provide additional evidentiary detail beyond what Section 5.1 requires. Where the Corporation elects to publish such a document, it shall meet the Corporation's whitepaper formatting standards and shall be added to the Corporation's research archive. Publication of such a document is not a precondition for publishing the underlying content, provided the sourcing standard in Section 5.1 is otherwise satisfied.

### 5.3 Corrections

The Corporation shall correct material factual errors in published content within 15 days of notification. Corrections shall be documented in the content record and noted in the content itself where practicable.

### 5.4 AI and Machine Learning Use in Published Content

The following standards govern the use of artificial intelligence and large language model ("LLM") tools in the production of any content the Corporation creates for publication on a social media platform, including but not limited to video content and video scripts, YouTube Shorts, and posts published on Bluesky, Medium, or any other social media platform ("Published Content").

**(a) No AI-authored Published Content.** No portion of any script, post, caption, or other Published Content shall be written, edited, or outlined by an LLM or other AI system.

**(b) Human-originated substance.** The thesis of each video shall originate with a human member of the production team, consistent with the Corporation's Phase 1 production process, which requires a written thesis to precede research. For Published Content other than video, the substantive content and any conclusions expressed shall likewise originate with a human.

**(c) No AI-generated images or music.** No image or music included in Published Content shall be generated by an AI system. If AI-generated material is inadvertently included, the Corporation shall use best efforts to identify and remove it upon discovery.

**(d) LLM outputs are not a citable source.** Outputs from LLMs or other AI systems shall not be treated as a citable source or evidentiary basis for any factual claim in Published Content or any accompanying research documentation, consistent with the sourcing standard in Section 5.1.

**(e) Primary source links.** Informational and educational Published Content shall include links to primary sources in the video description or post, as applicable, consistent with Section 5.1.

**(f) Scope.** This Section governs Published Content as defined above. It does not restrict the use of AI or LLM tools for organizational operations, governance, administrative tasks, or research assistance in the preparation of any research documentation the Corporation elects to publish under Section 5.2, provided any such use is disclosed as required by the Corporation's whitepaper formatting standards. A disclosed research-assistance use in a Materials and Methods section does not violate subsection (a), which is limited to Published Content itself.

## 6. Pre-Publication Review

Before any content is published, the Executive Director shall confirm that:

(a) all factual claims, facts, and figures satisfy the sourcing standard of Section 5.1;

(b) the seven-factor review under Section 4.2 has been completed and documented;

(c) if the content includes a policy intervention call to action, the CTA component requirement of Section 3.5 is satisfied;

(d) the content does not fall within the Restricted Period defined in Section 4.3, or if it does, Board review has been obtained;

(e) the content does not express support for or opposition to any candidate for public office; and

(f) the content complies with the AI and Machine Learning Use standards of Section 5.4.

The Executive Director shall escalate to the Board before publication any content that triggers factors (a) or (b) of the seven-factor test, is scheduled for publication during a Restricted Period, or the Executive Director has reason to believe may be in tension with this policy.

---

## 7. Reporting

The Executive Director shall include in the quarterly financial summary provided to the Board under Section 8.4 of the Bylaws:

(a) the lobbying expenditure log required by Section 3.6;

(b) cumulative lobbying expenditures against the applicable 501(h) limits;

(c) a summary of any seven-factor reviews completed during the quarter; and

(d) any content compliance issues identified during the quarter and their resolution.

---

## 8. Amendment

This policy may be amended by majority Board action pursuant to Article VII of the Bylaws. Material amendments affecting lobbying expenditure classification methodology or the political campaign intervention prohibition shall be reviewed by legal counsel before adoption.
