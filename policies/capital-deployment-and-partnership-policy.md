# CAPITAL DEPLOYMENT AND PARTNERSHIP POLICY

*restore280 Institute — Adopted by the Board of Directors by Written Consent, effective October 1, 2026*

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs restore280 Institute's (the "Corporation") capital facilitation activities and organizational partnerships. The Corporation's capital deployment function involves identifying climate solution opportunities where capital is the binding constraint, facilitating third-party capital toward those opportunities, and deploying the Corporation's own capital in furtherance of its exempt purposes. All capital deployment and partnership activities must further the Corporation's charitable purposes under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code and must not result in impermissible private benefit or private inurement.

Whether a given capital opportunity is selected for resourcing at all, and how much of the Corporation's resources it receives, is governed by the Strategic Prioritization and Resource Allocation Policy. This policy has no role in that selection decision. It governs only how a capital opportunity already selected under that policy is subsequently evaluated, approved, and managed for its capital- and partnership-specific requirements. It does not govern ongoing operational status reporting or Board oversight common to all Project types, which is addressed elsewhere in the Corporation's companion policies under Bylaws Section 10.2.

---

## 2. Exempt Purpose Alignment

### 2.1 Requirement

All capital deployment and partnership activity must further one or more of the Corporation's exempt purposes as stated in Section 1.2 of the Bylaws or preserve or increase resources available to the Corporation to further those purposes.

### 2.2 Prohibited Activities

Capital deployment and partnership activity shall not:

(a) result in net earnings inuring to the benefit of any director, officer, or other private person;

(b) provide more than incidental private benefit to any individual or entity;

(c) involve any transaction with a disqualified person that constitutes an excess benefit transaction under Section 4958 of the Internal Revenue Code; or

(d) jeopardize the Corporation's tax-exempt status.

---

## 3. Opportunity Identification and Evaluation

### 3.1 Evaluation Criteria

Before bringing any capital deployment opportunity to the Board, the Executive Director shall evaluate it against the following criteria:

**(a) Impact Clarity:** The degree of confidence that the deployment will produce a measurable reduction in atmospheric CO2 or advance another stated exempt purpose.

**(b) Capital Criticality:** Whether the opportunity would proceed without the Corporation's involvement, or whether capital is the binding constraint on execution.

**(c) Timeline:** The expected period between capital deployment and outcome realization.

**(d) Scalability:** Whether a successful deployment can be replicated or scaled, and to what total impact.

**(e) Execution Capability:** Whether the implementing partner has a demonstrated track record of executing comparable work.

**(f) Transparency:** Whether the partner is willing to document outcomes honestly and share data with the Corporation for research and publication purposes.

### 3.2 Disqualifying Factors

An opportunity shall not proceed if:

(a) the primary beneficiary of the deployment is a disqualified person under Section 4958;

(b) the implementing partner is unwilling to document and share outcomes; or

(c) the Executive Director cannot articulate a clear causal mechanism connecting the deployment to an exempt purpose outcome.

---

## 4. Approval Process

### 4.1 Third-Party Capital Facilitation

Where the Corporation facilitates capital deployment using only third-party funds, with no Corporation funds committed, the Executive Director may authorize the activity without Board approval, provided:

(a) the activity is consistent with an approved organizational strategy;

(b) the Executive Director documents the opportunity evaluation under Section 3.1; and

(c) the activity does not create any commitment of Corporation funds or contingent liability.

### 4.2 Corporation Funds

Deployment of the Corporation's own capital is subject to the expenditure thresholds established by the Board pursuant to Sections 8.2 and 8.3 of the Bylaws. All Corporation capital deployment at or above the unbudgeted expenditure threshold set by Board resolution requires Board approval pursuant to Article VII of the Bylaws before any funds are committed.

### 4.3 Required Documentation for Board Approval

Any request for Board approval of a capital deployment shall include:

(a) a written opportunity evaluation against the criteria in Section 3.1;

(b) a description of the implementing partner and the basis for confidence in their execution capability;

(c) the proposed terms of any partnership agreement;

(d) an outcome documentation plan; and

(e) a conflict of interest disclosure by any director or officer with a material relationship to the opportunity or partner.

---

## 5. Partnership Agreements

### 5.1 Required Terms

All capital deployment partnerships shall be governed by a written agreement that includes:

(a) a clear statement of the exempt purpose the partnership advances;

(b) the specific outcomes the partner is expected to achieve and the timeline for achieving them;

(c) the partner's obligation to provide outcome data to the Corporation for research and publication purposes;

(d) the Corporation's right to publish outcome data, in anonymized form where the partner requires confidentiality;

(e) representations by the partner that the partnership does not create private inurement or impermissible private benefit; and

(f) termination provisions if the partner materially fails to perform or if continued engagement would jeopardize the Corporation's exempt status.

### 5.2 Conflicts of Interest

No partnership agreement shall be executed with any entity in which a director, officer, or key employee has a material Financial Interest without: (i) full disclosure to the Board; (ii) the Interested Person's recusal from deliberation and approval; and (iii) a Board determination that the partnership is in the Corporation's best interest and on terms no less favorable than would be available from an unrelated party.

---

## 6. Outcome Documentation

### 6.1 Standard

The Corporation shall document the outcomes of all capital deployment partnerships with sufficient rigor to support the Corporation's research and publication function.

### 6.2 Required Records

For each capital deployment, the Corporation shall maintain:

(a) the original outcome predictions made at the time of approval;

(b) actual outcomes measured against those predictions;

(c) timeline predicted versus actual;

(d) capital efficiency (exempt purpose impact per dollar deployed);

(e) lessons learned, including what the Corporation would do differently; and

(f) any material deviations from the partnership agreement and their resolution.

### 6.3 Publication

The Corporation shall publish outcome documentation in its research output, subject to any confidentiality terms agreed with the partner. Disappointing or negative results shall be published; selective publication of positive outcomes only is not permitted.

---

## 7. Reporting

The Executive Director shall include in the quarterly financial summary provided to the Board under Section 8.4 of the Bylaws:

(a) cumulative capital deployed in the current fiscal year by category;

(b) outcome updates for completed or substantially completed deployments; and

(c) any conflict of interest issues identified during the quarter.

---

## 8. Amendment

This policy may be amended by majority Board action pursuant to Article VII of the Bylaws.
